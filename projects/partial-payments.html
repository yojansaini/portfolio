The Problem

Freelancers, contractors, delivery partners and project-based businesses operate on milestone logic: work happens in phases, payment follows completion. Yet every payment platform treats collection as a single event or a time-based split.

Existing solutions cover three things well: subscriptions for recurring fixed amounts, payment links for one-time collection, and basic partial payments for installment splitting. None of them answer a simple question: what happens when payment should only move when a deliverable is done?

The result is fragmentation. A contractor uses one tool to track project milestones, another to raise invoices, and a third to collect payment. None of these talk to each other. Disputes happen because payment terms were never formally agreed to. Late fees get applied without the customer ever seeing them upfront.

The insight: milestone-linked billing is not a payment problem. It is a contract and consent problem that happens to end in a payment.

The Feature

Partial Payments on SecurePay unifies project tracking, billing and collection into a single merchant-created order with three distinct modes.

Scheduled. Fixed installments on predefined dates. Standard partial payment behaviour.

Unscheduled. No fixed dates. The customer decides when to pay each leg, within the terms agreed at order creation. Flexible for variable project timelines.

Milestone. Payment legs are tied to project milestones. A leg only moves when the merchant marks a milestone complete, the customer accepts it, and the system confirms. This is the innovation.

Milestone Mode: How It Works

The merchant creates the order upfront, defining each milestone, the payment amount tied to it, any applicable late fees and penalties, and the consent terms the customer will see. None of this changes after the customer accepts the order.

When a milestone is complete, the merchant marks it done. The customer receives a notification and can either accept or dispute it.

If disputed, the merchant can respond and resubmit. This loop runs a maximum of two times. If unresolved after two cycles, SecurePay's internal team mediates and resolves the order, either voiding it with a full or partial refund per leg, or settling it based on the evidence.

Payment only moves after acceptance. No milestone acceptance means no collection for that leg.

The Hardest Product Decision

The most complex call was scoping customer consent cleanly within a merchant-created order, specifically around late fees and penalty clauses.

Merchants configure these upfront at order creation. That is the right design: terms are fixed before the relationship begins, not imposed mid-project. But it created a hard UX and compliance challenge. How do you surface fee and penalty terms to a customer in a way that is genuinely understood, not buried in fine print?

The answer was a dedicated consent layer at order acceptance. Not a checkbox, but an explicit breakdown of every fee, penalty rate, and trigger condition the customer is agreeing to before the first leg is ever collected.

Framework one
RBI PA Guidelines, 2025
Mandates a board-approved dispute resolution policy with clear refund timelines, and requires payment aggregators to implement transparent fee disclosure and fraud prevention systems.
Framework two
DPDP Act, 2023
Requires consent to be specific, informed, auditable and withdrawable, with affirmative action by the customer and no bundled or pre-ticked consents. RBI's proposed Business Conduct Directions, effective January 2027, embed the same standard.

Getting this right mattered because the late fee and penalty structure is exactly what generates disputes if it is not communicated clearly. Front-loading that transparency was both a compliance requirement and a product decision that directly reduces operational burden downstream.

Edge Cases Designed For

The three-dimension status chain, treating execution status, reconciliation status and refund status as independent chains, meant edge cases had to be handled without cross-contamination.

Missed milestone deadline. An OVERDUE state is triggered and a retry cadence is defined. A late fee is levied on the customer if the payment due date for that leg is crossed. A late penalty is levied on the merchant if milestone delivery is delayed past the agreed date. Both parties have the right to waive the respective charge for the other.

Failed leg after successful legs. Settled legs are never reversed. Only the failed leg is re-initiated. When an order is voided, the merchant can process a full or partial refund for any individual leg.

Chargeback on one leg. Isolated to that milestone leg. Other legs are untouched.

Dispute loop exhausted. Escalates to internal mediation after two cycles. The order is frozen during mediation.

What This Unlocks

Milestone mode is the first payment product in the Indian market purpose-built for project-based billing. Not an adaptation of subscriptions or payment links, but a native solution for how contractors, freelancers and delivery-based businesses actually operate.

The competitive gap is real. No existing payment aggregator or gateway offers milestone-linked collection with a built-in dispute and mediation layer, upfront consent capture for fees and penalties, and a three-status-chain architecture that keeps execution, reconciliation and refunds independent.
